International User Conferences

Coordinating a multi-region conference program, from regional planning and registration to main-stage production and live delivery.

← Creative & Design OperationsCase study

A shared program across regions

From 2018 to 2025, the user conference program brought together customers and partners across North America, the United Kingdom, Sweden, and Australia. The audience ranged from people who used the products every day to senior leaders. As the company expanded through acquisitions, the conferences helped attendees understand a growing product portfolio, learn from peers, and connect with the teams behind the products.

Each conference brought together different priorities. Regional leaders understood what mattered to their audiences. Marketing and sales had growth goals. The global team wanted the events to reflect a shared direction for the company. Delivering the program meant getting those groups to agree on what each event needed to accomplish.

Aligning priorities before production

Some regional leaders had spent years building events around brands that were now part of a larger organization. Their expectations reflected established relationships with customers and a sense of what made each event valuable locally.

We facilitated working sessions with regional leaders to find common ground across commercial goals, audience interests, and global priorities. Those discussions helped establish a shared framework for the program and gave regional teams room to shape what their audiences would experience.

Building an event brand that could travel

We developed an event branding system with a common visual foundation and room for regional adaptation. I directed its application across event websites, large-format graphics, motion, and presentation materials. The shared system made the conferences recognizable as part of one program while giving regional teams flexibility to reflect their audiences.

Getting registration live

Registration had its own deadline while planning for the live event was still underway. Internal stakeholders and external vendors were developing concepts on different schedules, and those decisions affected the event website, ticketing interface, and supporting assets.

I oversaw event website production and registration setup, coordinating the work from early concepts to a live, branded ticketing experience. At the same time, the wider team continued developing the program and preparing for on-site delivery.

Producing the main stage

I led production for the main-stage experience across audiovisual production, presentation design, and motion graphics. Each session brought together program requirements, speaker needs, creative assets, and venue capabilities. External speakers often had established presentation styles and specific requirements. We worked with them to accommodate those needs within the production plan and give the audience a coherent experience across sessions.

The stage anchored the conference narrative. It was where the organization shared its direction, introduced products, and invited customers and external speakers to contribute their perspectives.

Making space for customer conversations

A recurring on-site hub brought support teams, product specialists, and interactive demos into one space. Attendees could ask questions, explore products, and share feedback directly with the people behind them. That gave internal teams a way to hear how customers described their needs in conversation.

I also developed an interactive installation to help attendees understand how the company’s products and services fit together. Read the Interactive Digital Experience case study.

Reflection

The conferences gave customers a chance to see what the organization was becoming after a period of acquisitions. The work called for a shared direction that regional teams could make their own, and a delivery plan that connected early decisions about the audience and program to what people experienced on the day.

Project Credits

My scope also included presentation materials, print, merchandise, and sales enablement assets.

Collaboration
