Creative Center of Excellence

Building a unified creative function to strengthen governance, quality, and delivery across regions.

← Creative & Design OperationsCase study

The Starting Point

As the organization grew, creative work became increasingly distributed across teams and regions. Different ways of commissioning, reviewing, and delivering work contributed to inconsistent brand expression, fragmented workflows, and uneven expectations.

Internal partners lacked a clear understanding of the creative team’s services, how to submit requests, and what timelines to expect. Designers faced incomplete briefs, unclear approvals, and repeated revisions. Informal requests made demand harder to see and capacity harder to plan.

The opportunity was to establish a shared operating framework that connected creative expertise with clearer service expectations and delivery practices.

What Needed to Change

My focus was to establish the foundations of a Creative Center of Excellence: who the team supported, what services it provided, how work entered the system, how priorities were agreed, and how performance would be evaluated.

The aim was to build trust, predictability, and shared accountability while supporting consistent creative work across regions.

My Role

I led the strategy, research, facilitation, and implementation of the initiative, working with marketing leadership, regional stakeholders, cross-functional partners, and a distributed design team.

My responsibilities included developing the operating model, designing intake and workflow systems, facilitating collaborative workshops, and developing performance frameworks with the team.

Understanding the System

Research question

What might a clearer and more trusted creative function look like for internal partners in a growing organization?

I began by mapping stakeholders according to their relationship with the creative team and their involvement in requesting and delivering work.

This brought attention to internal partners beyond the team’s usual marketing relationships. Their requests were part of the workload, but they lacked a consistent entry point and shared expectations around scope, timing, and capacity.

The mapping helped define whose perspectives needed to inform the research and subsequent workshops.

Mapping the Current Experience

I developed internal client profiles informed by patterns observed through day-to-day collaboration. A focus group with the creative team explored delivery challenges, while stakeholder interviews examined how internal partners discovered the service, initiated requests, reviewed work, and experienced delays or uncertainty.

I synthesized these perspectives into a current-state journey map, connecting the internal client experience with the designer experience.

The findings informed Business Model Canvas workshops with key partners. Together, we examined services, stakeholder needs, value propositions, and operational relationships to establish a shared understanding of the creative function.

Designing for Clarity

Two connected workstreams translated the research into service improvements.

Structured intake

Working with designers and stakeholders, I developed dedicated request forms for creative work and digital support. The forms captured the information needed for scoping, prioritization, and planning, with submissions generating structured tasks in the project management platform.

This created a consistent starting point for requests and gave the team a clearer basis for discussing requirements and delivery expectations.

A shared service blueprint

Through collaborative working sessions, I developed a blueprint connecting client touchpoints with staff responsibilities, supporting technology, and management oversight.

It addressed how requests entered the service, how work progressed through review and delivery, and how feedback could inform ongoing improvement.

Building Ownership Around Metrics

I facilitated working sessions in which designers explored business priorities, reviewed proposed performance indicators, and helped define realistic measures of success.

The resulting framework connected creative quality and service delivery with shared expectations. It included indicators for project satisfaction, revision patterns, and file compliance, providing a basis for evaluating the service over time.

Outputs & Early Feedback

The initiative established three connected foundations:

  • A shared performance framework for the creative function.
  • Standardized intake and feedback forms, supported by workflow automation.
  • A service model connecting responsibilities, review stages, and improvement activities.

Survey feedback indicated greater confidence among internal clients in navigating the creative process, alongside a clearer sense of purpose and professional standing among designers.

These outputs and early feedback provided a basis for continued service evaluation and improvement.

Reflection

This work reinforced the importance of designing the conditions around creative delivery. Clear expectations, visible responsibilities, and shared ways of working help teams coordinate their expertise.

Research and participation were central to that process. Understanding both the internal client and designer experience helped connect operational decisions with the people expected to use the service.
