Website Consolidation & Governance

Leading the design workstream of a multi-region website consolidation, from setting shared objectives to delivering a design system regional teams could reuse.

← Creative & Design OperationsCase study

Why the websites needed to come together

As the organization grew through acquisitions, it kept regional brands and websites in place to preserve established customer relationships. Over time, customers encountered different navigation, messaging, and visual identities depending on which site they visited. Internally, teams maintained multiple platforms and content management systems, making updates and governance more difficult.

The consolidation set out to bring the regional sites onto a shared platform, make the combined product portfolio easier to navigate, and give regional teams room to address their audiences’ needs.

My role

I led the UX/UI design workstream and partnered with the Head of Digital, who reported to me, to align design priorities with technical implementation and regional launches. I helped define project objectives and owned the design roadmap, from discovery and information architecture through prototyping, asset production, documentation, and design quality reviews. Throughout delivery, I worked with digital and web development teams, marketing stakeholders, in-house designers, and an external agency to coordinate the design work with implementation.

Agreeing on objectives and regional needs

We co-facilitated discovery workshops with an external brand agency to understand each business’s customers, market context, and value proposition. We asked what each region needed to communicate and how those messages could fit within a shared website structure.

The workshops gave us a basis for negotiating which elements to standardize and where regions needed flexibility. Those decisions shaped the information architecture, navigation, and design priorities.

Turning priorities into a design roadmap

We prioritized two regional launches first. Their requirements gave us a starting point for defining reusable layouts, interface components, and interaction patterns. The patterns developed for those launches formed the baseline for subsequent regions.

The first two regions also needed product illustrations while the broader design system was still in development. We produced those assets in parallel so the launch designs could move forward as the shared system took shape.

Managing design delivery through change

I used sprint planning to sequence design work and coordinate dependencies with the digital and development teams. Stand-ups helped us track progress and surface blockers. Design reviews gave us a way to test navigation, content hierarchy, and messaging across regional scenarios before the work moved into production.

When the organization’s brand direction changed midway through delivery, we revisited the affected messaging and parts of the information architecture. We adapted those elements within the framework already in development and continued moving the regional sites toward launch.

Building governance into delivery

I maintained the shared design asset library and documented component patterns, layout principles, and design decisions. That gave designers and developers a common reference for implementation and quality reviews. It also gave teams a starting point for future regional pages and updates.

Multiple regional sites launched on the shared platform. They used a common approach to navigation and visual design while supporting regional content. Subsequent launches could build on the components, assets, and documentation already in place.
