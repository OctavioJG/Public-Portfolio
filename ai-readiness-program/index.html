Designing an Enterprise AI Readiness Program

From auditing existing tools to coordinating AI adoption and building capability across five regions.

← Research & StrategyCase study

The brief

Teams across five regions were experimenting with AI independently. Several were exploring similar ideas, and there was no shared way to evaluate where AI could help, what safeguards were needed, or which work should be coordinated.

I initiated, designed, and led an AI readiness program over approximately 18 months, working without a dedicated budget or team mandate. The work began in marketing and expanded to include use cases from sales, customer success, and business development.

Phase 1 · Audit the tools and map existing capabilities

I audited 18 tools in the marketing technology stack to identify which platforms offered AI capabilities, how teams were using them, and where those capabilities were underused. Most teams were using the default functionality, even where their existing tools could support more advanced workflows.

The audit identified gaps in training, workflow design, and governance. It also introduced Generative Engine Optimization as an emerging area and outlined what the organization would need to develop before pursuing it.

Phase 2 · Assess opportunities and procurement needs

I assessed three opportunities: internal GPTs to improve the speed and consistency of content production across regions, AI-assisted SEO within existing licences, and a potential upgrade from a scripted website chatbot to conversational AI.

For each opportunity, I assessed feasibility, modelled costs, and designed a pilot with timelines and success measures. I documented the security and compliance dependencies and recommended an ethics workshop before implementation so teams could discuss how AI would support their work. The recommendations prioritized capabilities already available under existing licences.

Phase 3 · Coordinate use cases across regions

As the organization shifted from a centralized model to regional operations, I gathered use cases from marketing, sales, customer success, and business development. I documented 16 use cases for the central team and captured additional proposals from regional stakeholders. Each assessment covered the problem, proposed use of AI, expected outcome, feasibility, and dependencies.

Comparing the proposals revealed where teams were independently pursuing the same solutions. I proposed where to use shared standards and where regions could make their own decisions. I also outlined a maturity scoring approach that leadership could use to track regional readiness over time.

Senior leadership approved the assessment, and the findings were shared with regional teams to guide coordination.

Phase 4 · Design the enablement program

I designed a program that began with remote preparation and culminated in a full-day workshop. The preparation gave teams time to agree on business priorities, map existing workflows, and practise writing prompts using real tasks.

The workshop design paired sessions on AI’s appropriate use, ethics, and transparency with hands-on work. Regional teams would present their own challenges and use a structured hackathon to test both incremental efficiency improvements and more substantial workflow changes.

The program was designed to produce a shared prompt library, documented workflows, early prototypes, and a pilot roadmap with metrics and owners. Senior leadership approved the design. It was ready for delivery when a reorganization phased out my role, and the workshop had yet to run.

Reflection

The audit showed how much the organization could do with tools it already had. The regional assessment surfaced a different decision about which use cases teams should develop together and which needed room for local experimentation. Those two findings shaped what I put into the enablement program and what teams would be ready to pilot afterward.

Project Credits

My contribution

Collaboration
