Contextual Research & Service Alignment

How field research helped a public-sector healthcare client and a product team agree on what to change.

← Research & StrategyCase study

The situation

A public-sector healthcare client was experiencing recurring difficulties with reporting software used across multiple sites. Everyday tasks took too many steps, and some teams relied on workarounds to get their work done. The client had raised these concerns before and was evaluating alternatives ahead of a contract renewal.

I led the design and delivery of a research engagement to understand where the friction came from and develop a plan the client and product team could act on together.

Preparing for the fieldwork

I started with a virtual focus group with the client’s core team to surface their assumptions and identify where we needed firsthand evidence. I planned the visit in close partnership with the client’s department lead, who helped determine which roles to include and how to organize the sessions.

The research focused on three questions. Where did users struggle? Which issues came from the software, its implementation, or onboarding? What could each team do to improve the experience?

What the research found

Some issues called for product development. Others came from how the software had been configured or introduced. A third group involved workarounds that had persisted even after updates addressed the original limitation.

One reporting task illustrated the difference. Users needed a level of configurability the software already supported, but the interface made that capability difficult to discover. Training could help current users complete the task, while product improvements could make the feature easier to find.

Building a shared roadmap

I shared preliminary findings during the engagement so the client and product team could discuss emerging priorities while the fieldwork was still underway. We built the roadmap together, identifying where the product team could make changes and where the client could improve implementation, training, or communication across sites. I then consolidated the findings and recommendations into a research report.

The client renewed its contract, and the teams implemented the roadmap. Two years later, I returned to interview and observe users again and assess how the changes had affected their work. The follow-up found improvements in how users experienced the software and gave the teams evidence to assess the changes against the original findings.

Reflection

The site visits changed what we prioritized. The same complaint could point to a missing feature, a configuration decision, or an established workaround. Watching people do the work helped us distinguish those causes and agree on who could address each one.

Project Credits

My contribution

I led research planning, fieldwork, and synthesis. I conducted interviews and contextual shadowing, facilitated co-design and roadmap workshops, and produced the research report and recommendations. I also conducted the follow-up evaluation.

Collaboration

The client’s department lead and core team helped shape the engagement. Regional administrators and specialist users contributed through interviews, workshops, and site visits. The product team partnered on roadmap priorities and follow-through.
