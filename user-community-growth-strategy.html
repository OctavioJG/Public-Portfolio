User Community Growth Strategy

What would it take to expand a valued customer community across regions?

← Research & StrategyCase study

Why this study was needed

Customer feedback consistently highlighted the organization’s user community as a reason for satisfaction. Members used it to exchange advice, learn from peers, and connect with product teams. Even some customers who reported problems with individual products valued the community.

An acquisition opened up the possibility of expanding into new regions. The teams responsible for expansion had different views of what the community should provide and how to judge whether a rollout was working. I led the study to establish what customers valued and what the organization would need to change before expanding.

How I conducted the research

I planned and facilitated focus groups with Sales, Marketing, Product, Customer Success, and Development to understand where their priorities converged and where they differed. I also interviewed existing customers, including highly engaged community members, and customers in prospective regions to see how their expectations compared.

I used a STEEP scan to examine social, technological, economic, environmental, and political forces shaping online communities. I then grouped session feedback by topic and tracked how often each topic came up.

What the research revealed

The community had value of its own. Positive feedback from customers who were frustrated with specific products suggested that the community supported loyalty independently of product satisfaction. Internally, teams described its purpose differently, which made it harder to agree on what to prioritize.

Localization mattered from the start. Customers expected regional content, familiar terminology, and local spelling conventions. Existing customer relationships and English-language content made the UK and Ireland the clearest starting point, with Australia as a second phase and the Middle East as a longer-term opportunity with more complex localization needs.

The platform limited what teams could deliver. The community ran on custom code maintained across several teams, with limited documentation and unclear ownership. Even updating regional spelling and terminology required substantial development coordination. The study identified that constraint as a prerequisite to address before expansion.

The strategic recommendation

I recommended moving to a purpose-built community platform and expanding in phases. The roadmap prioritized easier access and regionally relevant content first, with capabilities such as mobile access and user management sequenced around platform dependencies.

The study also explored ways to sustain growth, including bundling access with support, introducing membership tiers, and offering the community as a standalone service.

What the study set in motion

I presented the findings to the department that owned the platform. The study informed a subsequent organizational review, after which the organization separated support from community engagement. Support moved to a dedicated service platform, and the community moved to a purpose-built engagement platform.

Project Credits

My contribution

I led research planning, scheduling, facilitation, and synthesis. I developed the research visuals and presentation and presented the recommendations to the team responsible for the platform.

Collaboration
