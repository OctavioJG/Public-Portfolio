Technology Readiness & Business Strategy

A proposed five-year AI roadmap for internal operations, acquisition integration, and workforce readiness.

← Research & StrategyCase study

The brief

I developed this roadmap for a global technology company as part of MIT Sloan Executive Education’s Artificial Intelligence: Implications for Business Strategy program in 2023.

Growth through acquisitions had broadened the company’s software offering. Each acquisition also brought systems, contracts, and processes that teams had to reconcile. The company had begun consolidating internal platforms, and one product line already used machine learning. I explored where AI and automation could reduce recurring integration work while teams maintained day-to-day operations and the product team continued its cloud migration.

Where automation could help

One proposal was to update the acquisition playbook. Automation could separate standard contract clauses from unusual terms that needed specialist review. It could also help IT teams compare tools across the acquired and parent companies, identify duplicate licences, and flag upcoming renewals. These uses addressed work that came up with each acquisition.

The roadmap also proposed using customer relationship data to help account teams identify churn risk, evaluating how marketing assets contributed to business outcomes, and using a conversational assistant to handle routine enquiries and route complex cases to staff. The roadmap focused on internal operations, with AI features for the company’s products scoped as a separate R&D effort.

The proposed five-year roadmap

  1. Year 1 · Establish the foundation. Use regional focus groups and executive interviews to clarify priorities. Assess data readiness, define the resources and skills needed, and plan staffing so routine operations can continue during the transition.
  2. Years 2–4 · Introduce tools and build capability. Roll out selected initiatives alongside training and new technical roles. Update the acquisition playbook as teams learn which workflows can be automated and which decisions require specialist review.
  3. Year 5 · Evaluate results and guide investment. Revisit the original assumptions, compare results with baseline performance, and use the evidence to decide which initiatives to refine, expand, or investigate further.

Governance and workforce readiness

I proposed a multidisciplinary governance group to track progress, review data protection and bias risks, resolve issues, and report results. The workforce plan paired new roles in data and technical operations with upskilling and reskilling for existing staff. It also called for transparent communication about how roles could change and what training would be available.

The evaluation would compare time saved, work quality, and costs with historical baselines. It would also examine adoption and whether employees were able to spend more time on complex decisions once routine steps were automated.

Reflection

The acquisition playbook gave the roadmap a concrete test. If automation could help specialists review exceptions faster while maintaining the quality of their decisions, the company would have a basis for using it in future integrations. The proposal made that test explicit by connecting use cases to the people who would review the work and the evidence they would need to judge it.
